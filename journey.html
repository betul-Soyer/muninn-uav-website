    Blog

    Build log

    1. July 2026

      Team website online

      The website goes live with the tail-sitter architecture, the design decision log, the test programme and the mission profile. It is a scored SUAS deliverable and the public record of the campaign.

    2. Spring 2026

      Full mission inside 45 minutes — in simulation

      The complete SUAS mission — 3.2 km cruise, multicopter-mode search grids, SLAM mapping, target alignment and autonomous landing — ran end-to-end in the Gazebo digital twin, on a flight plan built from the rulebook's terrain and field data. It closed the largest open risk: the search trajectories fit inside the 45-minute window.

    3. Winter 2025–26

      Pre-trained models fail at 45 m — so we build our own dataset

      Component testing delivered a hard lesson: COCO-class pre-trained detectors could not reliably find people and tents from the mission's 45 m search altitude. The team built a custom aerial dataset and trained YOLOv8s on it. Re-tests at the target altitude showed high-accuracy detection, and decision #4 was written.

    4. Winter 2025–26

      Digital twin running in Gazebo

      The gogebakan VTOL model started flying in Gazebo, and the Baylands world was customized with person and tent models to mirror SUAS search scenarios. From this point on, every algorithm — search patterns, transitions, visual-servoing alignment — had to pass the twin before touching hardware.

    Method

    Test programme

    Component, sub-system and system stages in order. Each stage has written pass criteria; a stage that fails sends the design back rather than forward.

    Stage 1 · Component

    Perception at altitude

    The detection model is tested against the mission's real geometry: people and tents at 45 m. This stage exposed the pre-trained-model gap and validated the custom YOLOv8s replacement.

    Stage 1 · Component

    Bench & power

    Motors, ESCs, servos and the power system prove their numbers on the bench — thrust, current draw, temperatures — before integration.

    Stage 2 · Sub-system

    Digital twin in Gazebo

    The gogebakan model flies in a Baylands world customized with SUAS-style targets. Control logic, search patterns and transitions run here first.

    Stage 2 · Sub-system

    Visual-servoing alignment

    Camera-guided alignment over targets is verified in simulation: the aircraft repeatedly positions itself over the target and holds the alignment.

    Stage 3 · System

    Full mission on the clock

    A flight plan built from the rulebook's terrain data runs end-to-end in simulation — all tasks, one 45-minute window, no resets.

    Stage 4 · Field

    Hardware & flight testing

    Hardware integration, ground tests, then incremental flight testing: hover, transition envelope, and finally full mission rehearsals on the real airframe.

    Operations

    Procedures

    Excerpts from the documents every flight is run against.

    Preflight checklist (excerpt)

    1. Airframe walkaround — structure, elevons, prop integrity
    2. Battery voltage & secure mounting verified
    3. Control surfaces: correct direction, full travel
    4. GNSS lock & compass sanity vs. known heading
    5. Failsafe check: RC kill verified on the ground
    6. Geofence loaded and displayed at the GCS
    7. Crew roles confirmed: PIC, GCS, spotter
    8. Weather within limits (wind, visibility)
    9. Verbal go/no-go from all three crew roles

    Flight test card

    Every flight is flown against a one-page card written before we leave the lab:

    • Objective — the one question this flight answers
    • Configuration — airframe, firmware, parameters
    • Pass criteria — measurable, agreed in advance
    • Abort criteria — who calls it, on what trigger
    • Log review — findings recorded in the build log

    Battery handling (excerpt)

    • Charge only in fire-safe bags, never unattended
    • Storage voltage within 24 h after a test day
    • Cycle count and internal-resistance log per pack
    • Any puffed or dropped pack is retired — no debate
    • Field charging station at least 5 m from the flight line
