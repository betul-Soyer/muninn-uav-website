    GöğeBakan

    HSVTOL quadrotor tail-sitter · patented unified propulsion
    Entry for SUAS 2026 · Yazgit Community

    It takes off nose-up on its own tail, transitions to wing-borne cruise and flies the whole competition mission without a pilot: a 3.2 km waypoint route, multicopter-mode area search, SLAM mapping and precision target localization.

    Vehicle design Flight testing

    Engineering

    Design approach

    Every engineering decision on GöğeBakan is driven by four objectives: minimize mechanical complexity, optimize energy consumption, improve system reliability, and enable fully autonomous mission execution. That is why the aircraft is a tail-sitter whose four motors serve as a single unified propulsion system — the same rotors that lift it vertically pull it through cruise. No tilt mechanisms, no separate lift motors carried as dead weight, fewer points of failure. The architecture is patented.

    The same discipline runs through the software: low-level flight stabilization on PX4 is cleanly separated from high-level perception and decision-making on a ROS 2 companion computer. Everything is validated in a Gazebo digital twin before it flies. Read the design decisions, follow the test program, or meet the people behind it.

    The challenge

    The SUAS 2026 mission, task by task

    One aircraft, a 45-minute mission window, and every task flown autonomously — including takeoff, transitions and landing.

    Task 01

    Waypoint cruise

    A 3.2 km waypoint route flown in fixed-wing mode for maximum energy efficiency, inside strict airspace boundaries.

    Task 02

    Search & detect

    Over the search area GöğeBakan transitions to multicopter mode and flies a grid pattern at 45 m, detecting people and tents with a custom-trained YOLOv8s model.

    Task 03

    Map with SLAM

    The stabilized multicopter-mode imagery feeds SLAM algorithms that build a high-resolution orthomosaic map of the area.

    Task 04

    Align over targets

    Visual servoing holds the aircraft precisely over each confirmed target — perception and flight control agreeing in real time.

    SUAS is organized by RoboNation; the 2026 edition flies 14–17 September 2026. Scores combine mission performance with design documentation — including this very website (team handbook).
